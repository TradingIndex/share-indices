How it works

What we publish, when, what every number means, and what this is not.

Every weekday at 16:05 Beirut (9:05 New York), 30 minutes before the US open, we post the 5 liquid US stocks (price $10+, $50M+ traded a day) with the most unusual pre-market moves, on Instagram and in Daily lists. The next morning at 08:00 Beirut the website adds how each one really traded: closed, held or filled the gap, plus a 5-minute chart. Every Saturday the Movers Recap sums up the week on Instagram and in the Track record.

What is on this website

Daily listsEvery list exactly as posted, with the levels, a real 5-minute chart and the result of each stock. The Track record adds up every list since the start.
StatementsFor each company in our lists: what it does and why it gapped (earnings or not), its income statement, cash flow and balance sheet from SEC filings, and the CFA ratios (profitability, liquidity, solvency, efficiency, growth).
ValuationThree CFA valuation models on the same filings - discounted cash flow, residual income and dividend discount - plus a reverse DCF. They estimate what the company's own numbers support and compare it with the price: undervalued, near or overvalued by the models. Every assumption is shown and you can change it.

The numbers on each card

Gap %Pre-market price at 9:00 New York vs yesterday's close.
Pre-market priceThe last pre-market trade before 9:00 New York. Not live.
x normal daily rangeThe gap divided by the stock's average daily move over 14 days (ATR). Above 1x = bigger than a whole normal day.
Volume yesterdayYesterday's volume vs its 20-day average: was attention already building?
Yesterday high / low / closeThe first levels day traders watch after the 9:30 open. Back to the close = the gap is "filled".
20-day high / lowAbout one month's extremes: a breakout, or a level price may stall at.

How the 5 are chosen

Score = (gap ÷ normal daily range) × √(yesterday's volume vs normal). We tested 4 ways to rank on 3 years of data (238,631 gaps): this one picked the stocks that moved most compared with their normal day (2.3x) and traded the most volume (about 4x normal), and it did so every year.

Valuation in one paragraph

A company is worth the cash it will produce in the future, discounted for time and risk. The DCF projects free cash flow 10 years ahead and discounts it at the WACC (cost of equity from CAPM: 10-year Treasury + beta x 5% market premium, and the after-tax cost of debt). Residual income adds to book value whatever the company earns above what shareholders require. The dividend model values mature dividend payers. If the price is 20%+ below the middle model value we show "undervalued by the models", 20%+ above "overvalued by the models". A 1% change in growth or WACC can move the value a lot, which is why the calculator lets you test your own numbers.

What this is not

Not buy or sell signals. A big gap tells you there was news and attention. It does not tell you which way the price goes next. Pre-market prices are thin and change fast after the open. Traders typically check the news, watch the first 5-15 minutes after 9:30, and watch price around yesterday's close, high and low before deciding anything. Model values are estimates under stated assumptions, not price targets or ratings.
Educational information only, not financial advice and not buy or sell signals. A big gap shows news and attention, not direction. Day trading is risky and most day traders lose money. Prices: Yahoo Finance pre-market data at 9:00 New York; they can be incomplete or wrong.
