How the models work

1 · DCF - discounted free cash flow to the firm
FCFF = operating cash flow + interest x (1 - tax) - capital spending. Grown for 5 years at the recent revenue growth rate, fading over years 6-10 to a long-run rate, plus a terminal value FCFF11 / (WACC - g). Everything is discounted at the WACC (cost of equity and after-tax cost of debt, weighted by market value). Enterprise value - net debt = equity value; ÷ shares = value per share.
2 · Residual income - justified price-to-book
Value = book value per share + (ROE - r) x book / (r - g). A company is worth more than its book value only when it earns more on equity (ROE) than shareholders require (r).
3 · Dividend discount (Gordon growth)
Value = dividend x (1 + g) / (r - g). Used only for mature companies that pay out 30%+ of their earnings as dividends.
4 · Reverse DCF
Instead of asking what the stock is worth, asks what growth the current price already assumes. If the implied growth is far above what the company has ever done, the price expects a lot.
Cost of equity (CAPM)
r = risk-free rate (10-year US Treasury) + beta x equity risk premium (5%). Beta = how much the stock moved with the S&P 500 (2 years of weekly returns, Blume-adjusted).

Models are only as good as their inputs: one year of unusual cash flow, a recent acquisition or a cyclical peak can distort them. Data extracted automatically from SEC filings; check the original reports. Education only: not a price target, rating or recommendation.
